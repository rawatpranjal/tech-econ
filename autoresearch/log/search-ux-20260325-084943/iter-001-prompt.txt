## Three Quality Lenses

Judge everything through these lenses. They are deliberately vague — use your taste.

**Sexy**: Does this look like it was designed by someone who obsesses over pixels? Are transitions smooth? Do colors feel intentional? Is there visual rhythm? Would a designer screenshot this and share it?

**Intuitive**: Can a first-time visitor find what they need in 5 seconds? Do the suggestions, filters, and result layout guide the eye naturally? Is anything confusing or cluttered?

**Understands You**: When I search "causal inference for beginners", do the results FEEL curated for me? Does a paper look different from a package? Can I tell at a glance which result is the one I need? Does the system surface the right metadata to help me decide?

## Available Data (Most Is Unused!)

Each search result from the worker carries these fields. **Bold = currently NOT rendered** but available:

Rendered: name, description, tags, type, difficulty, category, prerequisites

NOT rendered: **authors, year, model_score, best_for, summary, semantic_cluster, content_format, depth_level, audience, use_cases, topic_tags, related_concepts, canonical_topics, topic, subtopic**

## CSS Conventions

- Use existing variables: `var(--bg-hover)`, `var(--accent)`, `var(--text-muted)`, `var(--text-primary)`, `var(--border-light)`, `var(--bg-card)`, `var(--accent-bg)`
- Dark mode: use `[data-theme="dark"]` selectors
- Animations: always add `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` fallback
- No hardcoded hex colors for anything theme-dependent
- Inline styles are OK for dynamic values like type badge colors
